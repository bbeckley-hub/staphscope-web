About the ESKAPE AMR Platform

Our Origin

The ESKAPE AMR Platform was born from a simple observation: researchers in low‑resource settings face significant barriers to using genomic surveillance tools. Existing web services are fragmented, require manual collation of results, and are often not optimised for the pathogens most relevant to Africa. I built the first pipeline, StaphScope as a command‑line tool to solve this. The overwhelmingly positive feedback from colleagues and the need for an even simpler interface led to this web platform.

Our Philosophy

We believe that access to advanced bioinformatics should not be limited by geography or institutional wealth. Therefore:

  • All pipelines are free and open‑source (MIT license).
  • Each pipeline is species‑optimised – no unnecessary analyses, no wasted compute.
  • The web interface requires no command‑line experience – upload FASTA files, get a complete report.
  • Data sovereignty is respected – your sequences never leave our server; you can also download the command‑line version to run offline.

Technical Overview

Under the hood, the platform uses:

  • Flask (Python) for the web interface.
  • Celery + Redis for asynchronous job processing – you can close your browser and receive an email when your analysis is ready.
  • Separate Conda environments for each pipeline – guaranteed reproducibility and no dependency conflicts.
  • Comprehensive databases: NCBI AMRFinderPlus, CARD, ResFinder, VFDB, PlasmidFinder, BacMet2, and many more.
  • Cloudflare Tunnel for secure public access while the platform is hosted on a personal laptop (soon to be migrated to institutional servers or cloud servers).

Impact & Vision

Currently, the platform is hosted on a personal laptop at the University of Ghana Medical School. Our immediate goal is to migrate to institutional servers or cloud servers, enabling 24/7 availability, larger file uploads, and concurrent user support. In the longer term, we aim to add pipelines for other priority pathogens (e.g., Salmonella, Neisseria), develop an API for programmatic access, and establish training workshops for public health laboratories across West Africa.

🤔 Wait… ESKAPE? As in “escape”?

Exactly. The ESKAPE pathogens are notorious for “escaping” the effects of antibiotics. But we think the name is also a global call to action:

  • Everyone must join forces – researchers, clinicians, policymakers, and citizens.
  • Smart surveillance is our first line of defence. No more guessing – we need genomic data.
  • Knowledge must be shared openly. No paywalls, no closed silos.
  • Africa bears a heavy AMR burden, but African solutions are already emerging.
  • Prevention is cheaper than cure. Let's stop resistant infections before they spread.
  • Every day we delay, more lives are at stake. The time to act is now, not tomorrow.

“We didn’t choose the name ESKAPE because it sounds cool (though it does). We chose it because it reminds us every single day: we must ESCAPE the AMR crisis – together, urgently, and with the best science we have.”

— Brown Beckley, lead developer (who secretly hopes this pun makes you smile, not roll your eyes)
